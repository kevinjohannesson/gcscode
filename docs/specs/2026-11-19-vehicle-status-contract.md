# Vehicle status owns its contract

Vehicle status defines what it shows about each vehicle, protocol extensions answer for their own vehicles, and the operator sees a small attention list: worst vehicle first, each with a level and a reason. It is the first contract under [ADR-0014](../decisions/ADR-0014-protocols-as-languages.md), so the map's contract and every later one will copy its shape.

Decisions are marked _(Owner.)_ or _(Controller.)_; the controller is the session that brainstormed this with the owner on 2026-11-19. _(Controller, in review.)_ marks a decision the controller took on 2026-11-20 on a finding of this spec's review, PR #60. _(Owner, after review.)_ marks one the owner took on 2026-11-22, on the controller's recommendation, after that review.

## Context

Today `@gcscode/extension-vehicle-status` is one footer line, `MAVLink: <mode> <lat>, <lng> <battery>%`, read straight from `MavlinkExports.telemetry` (`packages/extension-vehicle-status/src/vehicle-status-item.svelte`). It shows one vehicle and not whether it is armed.

That telemetry is one module-level store in `packages/extension-mavlink/src/telemetry-store.svelte.ts`. `applyMessage` reads only the message, never its header, so every MAVLink system and component writes over the others: mode and armed state, and position, attitude and battery too. The flight overlay and the map demo read the same store.

ADR-0014 Decision 2 says each feature owns its contract and protocols fill it in, that vehicle status's contract comes first, and that a feature "asks about one asset key at a time; only that key's source answers". The roadmap entry "Vehicle status owns its contract" adds the sender-identity fix.

The owner's research (`gcs-conflict-lessons-2026-11` and `gcs-human-factors-safety-2026-11`, kept outside this repo) shaped what the glance must carry:

- **Which vehicle.** Commands and alerts bound to the wrong vehicle caused at least one accident, and EASA's draft HMI memo (CM-HF-001) asks for a clear indication of which aircraft is meant.
- **Mode, and changes to it.** Mode changes the crew could not see or hear recur in investigated accidents, including a 2024 ArduPilot and Mission Planner case.
- **A level in green, amber or red,** ranked by urgency.
- **The link,** because link loss often does not appear as a warning at all.
- **Battery,** the largest single cause in UK small-drone loss-of-control reports.
- **A queue, not copies:** with several vehicles per operator, the view should rank who needs a human.

## Goals

1. **Vehicle status publishes its contract** as a service extension ([ADR-0012](../decisions/ADR-0012-service-extensions-vs-host-namespaces.md)): the vocabulary types from its entry point, and an API through which a protocol extension registers a status provider for its own source. _(Owner: "answer when asked".)_
2. **Each vehicle's status is facts plus a level.** The facts are a display name, the mode as the protocol's own text, whether the vehicle is armed, and its battery percentage. The level is one of three (green, amber, red) with a short reason in the protocol's own words. The link shows through the level and its reason ("link lost 12 s") rather than as a field of its own, so the contract carries no field that nothing shows. _(Owner: facts + level. Armed, and the link as level and reason: Controller.)_ Actions such as "return home" are not in this contract.
3. **The MAVLink extension answers for MAVLink vehicles,** one per system id, keeping state per system. It takes every input to a vehicle's status only from that system's autopilot component, the one whose HEARTBEAT has an `autopilot` other than `MAV_AUTOPILOT_INVALID`: mode, armed state and `system_status` from that HEARTBEAT, and the battery percentage and `BATTERY_STATUS` from that component's own messages. ArduPilot sends one `BATTERY_STATUS` per battery id, and the worst `charge_state` across them sets the level. That fixes the sender-identity defect for vehicle status. ArduCopter's modes keep their names; a vehicle with no mode table yet shows its mode number. _(Controller, in review.)_
4. **The level follows the vehicle's own judgment** where the vehicle reports one, and gcscode judges only what the vehicle cannot report about itself. _(Owner: "the vehicle's own".)_ In ArduPilot terms:

   | Signal                                                                                               | Amber                | Red                                 |
   | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | -------------------- | ----------------------------------- |
   | `BATTERY_STATUS.charge_state`; `LOW` and `CRITICAL` follow the vehicle's battery failsafe parameters | `LOW` or `UNHEALTHY` | `CRITICAL`, `EMERGENCY` or `FAILED` |
   | `HEARTBEAT.system_status`; ArduCopter reports `CRITICAL` when any failsafe has triggered             | —                    | `CRITICAL` or `EMERGENCY`           |
   | Time since that vehicle's last autopilot HEARTBEAT, measured by gcscode                              | 3 s                  | 10 s                                |

   The highest level wins, with its reason. Any other `charge_state`, such as `OK` or `CHARGING`, adds no level. _(`UNHEALTHY`, `EMERGENCY`, `FAILED` and other values: Owner, after review.)_ The 3 s and 10 s are _(Controller.)_ defaults; 3 s mirrors the owner's work GCS, whose light needed a ping under 3 s. A disarmed vehicle whose link goes quiet stays amber: it cannot fall, and a red that never matters teaches operators to ignore red, as the research's nuisance-failsafe cases show. _(Controller.)_ Nothing times assets out, so a vehicle switched off after landing stays amber until reload. That fires the `docs/out-of-scope.md` trigger of "Asset liveness", the first operator-visible stale entry, and this iteration declines it: `pnpm dev:sitl` runs one vehicle, and an asset timeout is an iteration of its own (see the propagation section). _(Controller, in review.)_

5. **The operator sees an attention list in a corner of the map:** vehicles worst first, shrinking to one line when all are green. A row shows the level, name, mode, an armed marker and the battery percentage, and an amber or red row adds its reason. It uses the map's existing `registerControl`, as the flight overlay registers into the map. _(Owner: mockup C, then "map corner + footer". Row contents: Controller.)_ Vehicles at the same level sort by name. _(Controller, in review.)_
6. **The footer keeps one line: the worst vehicle,** with its level, name, mode, battery and reason, plus how many others are red, amber and green, so a second red is never hidden. A red still shows when the map is hidden behind a maximized panel. _(Owner. Counting all three levels: Owner, after review.)_
7. **A mode change flashes,** in both places, with the previous mode and how long ago it changed. gcscode sends no mode commands yet, so every change is un-commanded from its point of view and every change flashes. _(Controller.)_ The provider keeps the history, so the contract in the first PR already carries the previous mode and when it changed. A change is a mode that differs from the last one heard from that vehicle, including one first heard after the link returns, since a failsafe may have switched it while the link was quiet. The first mode heard from a vehicle is not a change. _(Controller.)_ The footer flashes only a change of the vehicle it shows. _(Controller, in review.)_

## Non-goals

- **Actions** ("return home", "locate"), and the guards and per-vehicle binding the research asks of commands. They come in a later contract.
- **A floating surface outside the map.** Parked: see the propagation sections.
- **Lost-link behaviour** ("if the link drops now, this vehicle will …"), sound on alerts or mode changes, and which vehicle the operator commands. gcscode has no commands yet.
- **The flight overlay's and the map demo's reads** of the shared store. They keep the mix-up until the map gets its contract.
- **ADR-0014's other follow-ups:** `airframe` leaving `extension-assets`, never-reissued keys, and the `mavlink` import rule.
- **Position in vehicle status.** The map shows where vehicles are; latitude and longitude leave the footer. _(Controller.)_
- **Running several SITL vehicles** from `pnpm dev:sitl`.

## Design

**The contract** lives in `@gcscode/extension-vehicle-status`'s entry point: a `VehicleStatus` type with the facts, level and reason of Goal 2; a status provider type; and a `VehicleStatusApi` whose `registerStatusProvider(sourceId, provider)` returns a `Disposable`. One provider per source id; a second registration on a live id throws, as `createSource` does. A provider is asked once per asset, with that asset's `RegisteredAsset`, and answers every asset of its source with a reactive object that stays current, so vehicle status never polls a provider. "Once" means no polling, not a cached first answer: the object's status stays absent while the asset is not a vehicle and appears when it becomes one, so a vehicle whose companion computer or gimbal is heard before its autopilot is listed as soon as the autopilot is. Vehicle status asks again only for a key that is new to it or when a source's provider changes, and keeps nothing for a key that has left `getAssets()`, since keys are still reissued (Known unknowns). _(Controller, in review.)_ MAVLink leaves the status absent for a system that has never sent an autopilot HEARTBEAT, such as another ground station or an ADS-B receiver, which would otherwise sit red at the top of the list forever. _(Controller.)_

**The provider's clock.** Goal 4's heartbeat-age row turns amber and red while nothing arrives, so the provider runs one clock, for all its vehicles rather than one per vehicle. MAVLink creates its provider and starts its clock in `activate()`, and pushes the provider, clock included, to `subscriptions`, so containment stops both after a failed activation (`packages/shell/src/extension-host/registry.ts`). Until it holds a registration, the provider looks up vehicle status's API on every tick and registers when it finds it. That needs no traffic, which matters because `packages/extension-mavlink/src/mavlink-client.ts` opens one socket and never reconnects: the provider registers whether vehicle status comes up at boot, is enabled later in the session or comes back after a failed activation, and with mavlink2rest down it is registered in time to report its source's state. Its message handling sits behind its own disposed guard, as `packages/extension-mavlink/src/asset-adapter.ts`'s does, since the WebSocket is not a subscription and keeps delivering after a failed activation. _(Controller, in review.)_

**Across vehicle status's own re-enable.** Provider registrations survive vehicle status's disable and re-enable, as `mapApi`'s registrations survive the map's: the provider table is module-level, and its entries are registrations whose disposables their providers own, the case ADR-0012's Mechanics let a service keep. A provider therefore registers once, its `Disposable` stays valid until the provider disposes it, and re-enabled vehicle status asks the providers already registered. While vehicle status is torn down, `host.extensions.getExtension` returns no exports, so a provider that resolves the API lazily finds nothing and tries again on its next tick (The provider's clock); a registration through an API reference captured earlier lands in the same table and is asked once vehicle status is enabled again. _(Controller, in review.)_

**When a source fails.** Vehicle status reads each source's answers inside a guard of their own where it ranks them, in the list and in the footer, so a provider that throws becomes one red "status unavailable" line for its source that ranks like any red row, and one `<svelte:boundary>` around the list and one around the footer item catch the rest, so a throw never breaks the map view or the status bar. It is red because it hides the status of every vehicle in that source and means code is broken. An absent Assets service shows its own line, so an empty list never reads as "no vehicles". _(Controller, in review.)_

**Which vehicles exist** comes from `AssetsApi.getAssets()`. Vehicle status routes each asset to the provider registered for its `sourceId` and lists the assets that have a status. An asset whose source has no provider shows its `sourceLabel` with no level. `RegisteredAsset` carries no name: a vehicle's display name is a fact of this contract, which its provider supplies (Goal 2), and under ADR-0011 the label renders, never an id. _(Controller, in review.)_

**A source's own state.** A provider may also report its source's state as a level and reason, such as "no connection to mavlink2rest". Vehicle status shows that line whenever its level is not green, and when the source has no vehicles, so "can't hear anything" never reads as "no vehicles", and a stopped mavlink2rest shows its cause beside its vehicles' "link lost". _(Controller, widened in review.)_ The provider supplies that line's label, such as "MAVLink", since a source with no assets has no `sourceLabel`, and under ADR-0011 a `sourceId` never renders. _(Controller, in review.)_ In the list and in the footer, a source's line ranks like a vehicle row at its level, as "status unavailable" does (When a source fails), so with mavlink2rest down the footer's one line shows the source line rather than "no vehicles". _(Controller, in review.)_

**The provider finds its own vehicle.** It is asked with the `RegisteredAsset`, whose `localId` is the id its source registered, so it finds its system by equality, which ADR-0011 allows for identity. `AssetSource.registerAsset` stays as it is, and no key-to-system map needs rebuilding after a re-claim. _(Controller, in review.)_

**MAVLink** keeps a status per system id, adds `BATTERY_STATUS` to its mavlink2rest filter, and registers its provider. The shared telemetry store stays as it is for its other readers.

**Activation order.** MAVLink needs vehicle status's API to register, and vehicle status needs the map's to place its list. A registrant resolves the service it writes into in one of three ways:

- **Eagerly:** it resolves at `activate()` and depends on `bundledExtensions` order. If the service is inactive then, it degrades and stays degraded until it is itself re-enabled, even after the service activates. The flight overlay does this.
- **Driven by traffic:** it re-resolves on each inbound message, as the asset adapter does.
- **On a recurring path of its own:** a registrant whose own code runs again may resolve lazily on that path. MAVLink's provider resolves vehicle status on its clock (The provider's clock), and vehicle status resolves the map on a clock of its own (below), neither during `activate()`, so no activation-time edge forms between them.

ADR-0012's Mechanics name the first two and say a registrant "with nothing to wait for cannot resolve lazily at all". The third is this spec's addition, which the first PR records in ADR-0012 as a breadcrumb (Breadcrumb in ADR-0012, and the extension API README). _(Controller, in review.)_

**Vehicle status's map clock.** In `activate()`, vehicle status starts a clock and pushes it to `subscriptions`. On each tick, until it holds a registration, it looks up the map's exports, and once it finds them it registers its list through `registerControl`, pushing that `Disposable` to `subscriptions` too. A map that activates at boot, later in the session or after a failed activation gets its list on the next tick, and until then the footer still works. The registration survives the map's own disable and re-enable: `mapApi` is a module-level registry that outlives them, the map's `activate()` never clears its controls, and `packages/extension-map/src/map-view.svelte` renders every registered control each time it mounts (`packages/extension-map/src/map-api.svelte.ts`). So the clock stops looking once it has registered. This timer only looks for the map, so vehicle status still never polls a provider. Vehicle status keeps its place in `bundledExtensions`, and `packages/shell/src/extension-host/bundled-extensions.test.ts` stays as it is. Because the list registers on a tick rather than in `activate()`, it fires neither `docs/out-of-scope.md` trigger that an activate-time registrant would: "Extension activation ordering / dependency declaration" and "Cleanup of contributions an extension registered into another extension's registry". The flight overlay is still the only extension that resolves and registers into another's registry in `activate()`. _(Owner, after review: this clock route replaced option (a).)_

The trade the owner chose, against the two other routes:

- **Against (a),** moving vehicle status after the map and resolving it eagerly: one timer whose only job is to find the map, instead of a reorder, a changed bundled-extensions test, two re-deferred `docs/out-of-scope.md` rows and a list that never came back when the map was enabled later.
- **Against (b),** registering when the map's exports appear: the same result, without the first reactive root in a gcscode package.

The cost is that timer, in an extension the design otherwise keeps from polling. _(Owner, after review.)_

**Left to the builder:** exact type and member names; how a vehicle is named (the protocol supplies it; camera-sim already calls MAVLink system 1 `drone-1`); which map corner; the flash's look and length; the footer's wording with no vehicles.

## Known unknowns

- **Thresholds.** The 3 s and 10 s link thresholds are defaults, not measurements. Revisit if amber flickers in normal SITL flight.
- **Battery failsafe in SITL.** `charge_state` turns `LOW` only when the vehicle's battery failsafe is configured. The hand check confirms what SITL's defaults report, and the PR body says how to make it go amber.
- **Several vehicles.** `pnpm dev:sitl` runs one vehicle, so ordering and counts are covered by tests with a fake provider, and the hand check covers one real vehicle.
- **The link down at start.** Those tests use a fake provider, so the hand check also opens the page before mavlink2rest is up and confirms that the footer shows the MAVLink source line, not "no vehicles" (PR #60 RT-21).
- **Registry re-activation.** Keys restart when Assets re-activates, until the `airframe` follow-up makes them never reissued. Vehicle status keeps nothing for a key that has left `getAssets()`, and the provider finds its system by `localId` (Design), so neither holds a key that could come to name another system.
- **Rows that look alike.** A source with no status provider shows each of its assets as its `sourceLabel`, so several of them look alike. No source does this today: MAVLink, the only one, registers its provider on its clock's first tick after boot, so only an asset heard before that tick shows without one, and only until the tick. Revisit when a second protocol's assets have no status provider.
- **Disabling MAVLink mid-flight.** Its source is disposed, so its vehicles leave the list instead of turning red. That is the operator's own action and stays as is; revisit if it ever happens by accident.
- **What the first PR's propagation names.** The ledger amendment ("Lazy resolution of a service dependency") and the ADR-0012 breadcrumb land with the first PR, but name vehicle status's map list, which only the second PR builds. The first PR's two edits name only MAVLink's status provider, and the second PR adds the map list to both when it merges (PR #60 RT-26, PR #60 EA-18). _(Controller, in review.)_
- **Who owns the list's registration.** Vehicle status's map clock, as the Design reads, pushes the list's `Disposable` to `subscriptions` after `activate()` has returned. That works because the host keeps the array by reference (`packages/shell/src/extension-host/registry.ts`), but no extension does it today and no registry test covers it. The second PR's plan carries the fix: the map clock holds the list's registration and disposes it together with itself, as MAVLink's provider owns its registration, so nothing is pushed to `subscriptions` after `activate()` returns (PR #60 RT-27). _(Controller, in review.)_
- **Comments in `bundledExtensions`.** Vehicle status's comment in `packages/shell/src/extension-host/bundled-extensions.ts` says it reads MAVLink's exports per use, which the first PR ends. The first PR rewrites it to say that MAVLink's provider finds vehicle status on a clock, and the second PR adds that vehicle status finds the map on a clock of its own. The first PR also makes map-demo's comment, which opens "Same as vehicle-status", stand on its own (PR #60 RT-28). _(Controller, in review.)_
- **Polling for another extension's exports.** MAVLink's provider and vehicle status's map clock both poll `host.extensions.getExtension` for another extension's exports, where VS Code fires `extensions.onDidChange`. The VS Code alignment table does not record that, and the ledger trigger the amendment keeps cannot be fired by more polling. When the second PR merges, the alignment row adds `extensions.onDidChange` and a trigger the pattern can fire: the first extension besides these two that polls for another's exports. The trade names a `host.extensions` change event as the route not taken, at the cost of an `@gcscode/extension-api` change that would land first (PR #60 EA-16). _(Controller, in review.)_
- **What `MapApi` promises across the map's re-enable.** The map clock stops once it has registered (Design) because the list's registration survives the map's disable and re-enable, which rests on map internals that `MapApi` does not state. The second PR states on `MapApi` that registrations survive the map's disable and re-enable, as ADR-0012 asks each service to, and cites that statement where the clock stops (PR #60 EA-17). _(Controller, in review.)_
