# Manual control through the station

The TX16S's mapped sticks reach a SITL vehicle as MAVLink `MANUAL_CONTROL` frames that the station process sends, addressed to the one vehicle the operator named, while the radio's held control switch is on. This is the amendment Linear GCS-39 asks for: it rewrites the unmerged manual control v0 draft (`spec/manual-control-v0`, `docs/specs/2026-11-22-manual-control-v0.md` on that branch) to [ADR-0016](../decisions/ADR-0016-one-station-process.md)'s one station process and to GCS-40's mapping profile, and splits it into the Manual control lane's rungs.

Decisions are marked _(Owner.)_ or _(Worker.)_. The worker is the session that wrote this on 2026-11-25 while the owner was away. Each _(Worker.)_ decision is a default the owner can overturn in review.

## Context

### What changed since the v0 draft

The v0 draft gave manual control a sidecar of its own, `packages/manual-control-sidecar`, which read the radio, picked the first autopilot it heard and sent `RC_CHANNELS_OVERRIDE` and a heartbeat as system 255. Three things have since replaced parts of it:

- **[ADR-0016](../decisions/ADR-0016-one-station-process.md).** One station process sends everything gcscode sends to a vehicle, with one MAVLink identity and one heartbeat, and it is the only place a target vehicle is chosen (Decisions 1, 3). Manual control is a part of that process, with its own thread and fixed deadlines, not a process of its own (Decision 4). The draft's sidecar, its heartbeat and "the first autopilot heard" all go.
- **The station exists.** `packages/mavlink-station` (`gcscode-station`, GCS-38 and GCS-29) reads the radio with [`libs/radio-input`](../../libs/radio-input/README.md), takes a snapshot every 20 ms on its radio side, which owns SDL, and serves it to the page on `ws://127.0.0.1:8091/ws` ([README](../../packages/mavlink-station/README.md)). It sends nothing to any vehicle yet.
- **The mapping layer (GCS-40, PR #82).** A profile, such as the bundled [`gcscode-adv.toml`](../../libs/radio-input/profiles/gcscode-adv.toml), names what each input means: axis roles `roll`, `pitch`, `throttle`, `yaw` and `aux1` to `aux4`, each calibrated; on/off roles `control` and `rtl`, which may map only to a held button; and Pulse switches with named positions. The draft's fixed Classic channel table goes, and with it the draft's "channel 16 is the control switch": the control switch is the profile's `control` role.

### What ArduPilot does with `MANUAL_CONTROL`

From ArduPilot's master source, read on 2026-11-25 (`libraries/GCS_MAVLink/GCS_Common.cpp`, `ArduCopter/GCS_MAVLink_Copter.cpp`, `libraries/AP_Arming/AP_Arming.cpp`, `libraries/RC_Channel/RC_Channels.cpp`):

- It takes a `MANUAL_CONTROL` only from a system id it counts as its GCS (`sysid_is_gcs`, the `MAV_GCS_SYSID` range, 255 by default) and only when the frame's `target` is its own system id. Anything else is dropped without a word.
- Copter maps four axes onto its roll, pitch, throttle and yaw channels, which follow `RCMAP_*`: `y` to roll and `r` to yaw over -1000 to 1000, `x` to pitch, reversed, over -1000 to 1000, and `z` to throttle over 0 to 1000. A negative `z` drops the whole frame. Each axis is scaled into that channel's own `RCn_MIN` to `RCn_MAX`, so the vehicle's RC calibration applies, not the radio's PWM. `INT16_MAX` in an axis releases it: ArduPilot sets that channel's override to 0, which counts as no override, so the channel goes back to the receiver at once. Buttons and the extension axes are ignored by Copter.
- Each axis becomes an RC override, which holds until `RC_OVERRIDE_TIME` (3 s by default) passes with no new one, then lapses to the vehicle's receiver. A vehicle with no receiver reaches its RC failsafe after `RC_FS_TIMEOUT` (1 s), which can fall inside that hold; GCS-43 measures how the two meet on SITL.
- A `MANUAL_CONTROL` also counts as a heartbeat from the GCS for the GCS failsafe.
- Copter arms on right yaw with the throttle at zero, and disarms on left yaw, held for 3 s (`RC_Channels::rudder_arm_disarm_check`; `ARMING_RUDDER`, arm or disarm by default on Copter). A single frame is held for the default 3 s, just as long, and a stream or a longer `RC_OVERRIDE_TIME` holds the pose as long as the sticks do.

### The lane's rungs

The Manual control project in Linear climbs: GCS-41, one control frame to a disarmed SITL seen on the wire; GCS-42, a fixed-rate stream, rate and jitter measured; GCS-43, unplugging and replugging the radio never makes the vehicle jump; GCS-44, flying one SITL vehicle by hand in Loiter. GCS-96 replays a recording through the running station so those checks can repeat without a hand on the sticks. Before GCS-41 can start, the station must heartbeat (GCS-30), which needs its system id (GCS-28), and GCS-40 must pass its smoke test. The steady core's criteria the tickets name (M1 to M7, F5) are the owner's, kept outside this repo; this spec cites them by id and leaves their numbers there.

## Goals

1. **`MANUAL_CONTROL`, not `RC_CHANNELS_OVERRIDE`.** _(Worker.)_ The station sends the four sticks as `MANUAL_CONTROL`'s `x`, `y`, `z` and `r`, and nothing else. See [the decision](#decision-manual_control-not-rc_channels_override).
2. **A frame is built from the mapped controls, never from raw axes.** `y` is roll, `x` pitch, `z` throttle and `r` yaw, each the mapper's calibrated value scaled to the frame's range and rounded: -100% gives -1000, 0% gives 0 and 100% gives 1000, and throttle's 0% to 100% gives 0 to 1000. The station scales the mapper's unrounded value, not the whole `percent` the page shows, so a frame keeps the stick's full resolution. `buttons` is 0. A profile that leaves out any of the four sticks never takes control, and the station says so when it opens the radio.
3. **One named target.** _(Worker.)_ The station sends control frames to one vehicle, named by its MAVLink system id with `--control-vehicle <id>`, which `pnpm dev:sitl` passes through. With no `--control-vehicle`, the station sends no control frame and says so once at start. It addresses a frame only to that id, and, release frames aside (Goal 5), only while that vehicle is heard: a `HEARTBEAT` from it with an autopilot other than `MAV_AUTOPILOT_INVALID` in the last 3 s, on the station's own MAVLink link, which GCS-30 brings. Choosing the target from the page is a rung of its own, before GCS-44 ([rungs](#rungs)).
4. **The control switch is the profile's `control` role, and it never takes control by surprise.** Control has four states. Off, Waiting and On come from the v0 draft; Sticks out is new:
   - **Off**: the switch is off, no radio is open, no profile maps it, the target is not heard or none is named.
   - **Waiting**: the switch is on, but has not been seen off since the station started, the radio was opened or the target was heard again. A start, an opened radio or a target heard again with the switch on leads here, whatever the sticks. Only the switch going off, then on, leaves it.
   - **Sticks out**: the switch moved from off to on with the radio mapped and the target heard, but the sticks are not safe. The station prints which stick is out (`control: sticks out, throttle at 40%`). It goes On once the sticks are safe, with the switch still on.
   - **On**: the switch moved from off to on, or control was Sticks out, with the radio mapped, the target heard and the sticks safe.

   The sticks are safe when throttle is at most 5% and roll, pitch and yaw are each within 10% of centre. _(Worker.)_ So a replug, a restart or a reheard target never takes control without the switch moving, and the switch moving never sends a deflected stick: the jump GCS-43 forbids. Throttle low is the safe pose for a vehicle on the ground, which is all GCS-41 to GCS-43 use; GCS-44 decides what is safe for taking control in flight, where a low throttle descends.

   Turning the switch off always leads to Off. From Off, only the switch moving to on leads anywhere else, to Sticks out or On; a lost radio or an unheard target in any state leads to Off, and its return with the switch on to Waiting. A profile that maps no `control` never leaves Off. Each event in this list is a test case. The station prints each change of state on its terminal (`control: on, vehicle 1`).

5. **A vanished radio stops the frames; a deliberate stop releases the sticks.** When the radio vanishes, control goes to Off within one control tick (40 ms) and the station sends nothing more: the owner's answer on GCS-43, "stop sending and hand over to the vehicle's failsafe". The vehicle holds the last frame until `RC_OVERRIDE_TIME` lapses, then its receiver or RC failsafe takes over. _(Worker.)_ When control leaves On any other way (the switch goes off, the target is no longer heard, the station is told to stop with SIGINT or SIGTERM, or the control thread panics), the station sends release frames, all four axes at `INT16_MAX`, on every tick for one second, to the last target whether it is heard or not, then nothing. Going On again within that second ends the releases. A release hands the sticks back at once to a receiver; a vehicle with none keeps its last values and reaches its RC failsafe `RC_FS_TIMEOUT` after the last real frame, release or not. The station never sends a neutral frame. See [the decision](#decision-a-vanished-radio-stops-a-deliberate-stop-releases). Release frames come with the stream, from GCS-42: GCS-41's single frame to a disarmed vehicle lapses by itself.
6. **No frame in ArduPilot's stick arming pose, until GCS-44 decides arming.** _(Worker.)_ While On, the station sends no frame whose throttle is under 5% while yaw is beyond ±50%, and prints why (`control: not sent, throttle low with yaw at 82% arms or disarms ArduPilot`). GCS-41 to GCS-43 run against a disarmed vehicle that must stay disarmed; a stream in that pose, or one frame under a longer `RC_OVERRIDE_TIME`, would arm it. Leaving the pose sends the next frame as usual. GCS-44 keeps, narrows or lifts this when it decides how the operator arms.
7. **The page's liveness never starts, stops or delays control.** _(Worker.)_ Manual control runs from the radio through the station's radio side and its control thread to the vehicle. Closing, freezing, hiding or reloading the page changes nothing: the control switch decides ([ADR-0015](../decisions/ADR-0015-native-sidecars.md) Decision 2). Deliberate configuration is another matter: the page choosing the target, a watch-only setting or a vehicle class's data may change control once the station acts on page messages. Disabling the MAVLink or Manual control extension is configuration, but no page message reaches control yet, so for now it changes nothing; the rung that first lets the page choose the target decides whether a disable releases control. A disable closes the extension's station connection, as a reload or a closed tab does, and those never stop control, so a disable can reach control only as a message sent before the close, never as the close itself.

   This answers ADR-0016's open "when manual control stops" for manual control. It also binds the heartbeat's half: ArduPilot counts every `MANUAL_CONTROL` as a GCS heartbeat, so from GCS-42 on, while control is On, the vehicle's GCS failsafe stays fed whatever the page does. GCS-30's rule for when the heartbeat stops decides the vehicle's GCS failsafe only while control is Off, and GCS-30 carries that. The page protocol does not change for GCS-41.

8. **A build that links the radio player sends control frames only to a vehicle it has shown is SITL.** _(Worker.)_ That covers the replay binary and every test that replays a recording into the station. A test sends control frames only to a fake vehicle of its own, a UDP socket it reads, never to the dev stack. This settles the question GCS-41 carries from the [virtual radio spec](2026-11-24-virtual-radio.md#known-unknowns). See [the decision](#decision-a-replay-build-refuses-a-vehicle-it-cannot-show-is-sitl).

## Non-goals

- **A real vehicle.** Until a failsafe rung, manual control is for SITL. The station's README and every view that shows control say "SITL only". Only a replay build checks it (Goal 8); the shipped build trusts the operator, as the v0 draft did.
- **The aux axes, the switches and `rtl`.** Flight modes and actions are commands, which the commands lane brings (GCS-48 first). The radio's other inputs reach the page as today and go nowhere else. GCS-44 changes mode by MAVProxy's console until a command rung lands.
- **Gamepads and other radios.** `MANUAL_CONTROL` suits them (Goal 1), and they join when the mapping layer reads a second kind of controller.
- **Choosing the target from the page, and two pages.** The page sends the station nothing, so the open questions of how the page names a vehicle and what two pages at once do ([ADR-0016](../decisions/ADR-0016-one-station-process.md#consequences)) stay with the rung that first lets the page choose.
- **The station's system id, its heartbeat and its MAVLink link.** GCS-28 and GCS-30. This spec only needs that id to be one the vehicle counts as its GCS.
- **Vehicle classes.** _(Worker.)_ Throttle as `z` 0 to 1000 (Goal 2), the arming pose (Goal 6) and the safe sticks (Goal 4) are ArduCopter's answers to [ADR-0018](../decisions/ADR-0018-vehicle-classes-as-selectors.md)'s class questions, held in the station without class data. These rungs are ArduCopter-only and read no class data, so disabling a class add-on changes nothing yet. The first rung that sends to another class, or GCS-44 if it keeps Goal 6, takes them from class data (ADR-0018 Decision 7), and is the first takeover that decision governs. Until then they are stopgaps until N14.
- **Windows and Linux timing.** The control thread's timing protection is macOS's first, as in the v0 draft (`NSActivityLatencyCritical`); other platforms log that they have none.

## Design

### Where it lives

Manual control is a part of `packages/mavlink-station`, as ADR-0016 Decision 4 says. It has three pieces, and only the last knows MAVLink:

- **The radio side**, as today: it owns SDL, takes SDL's events for up to 20 ms, then takes a snapshot of the raw inputs and the mapped controls, and publishes it into the page's `watch` channel only when it changed (`send_if_modified`, `src/input.rs`). Three changes: the mapped controls gain the mapper's unrounded stick values next to `percent`; on every tick, changed or not, the radio side stores the tick's count in an atomic of its own, a liveness mark that only the control thread reads, so a radio held still is told apart from a stalled radio side while the page's feed stays as protocol 1 describes it; and before it publishes, it checks SDL's queue for a removal of the open radio and, if one is queued, handles it first ([Frames the station never sends](#frames-the-station-never-sends)).
- **`control`**: the state machine of Goal 4, the safe sticks, the arming pose of Goal 6, and the four stick values, in the profile's words. It knows no protocol.
- **The MAVLink link**, which GCS-30 brings for the heartbeat: it knows which vehicles it hears and builds and sends `MANUAL_CONTROL`. What the control thread needs from it, which GCS-30 carries: a send and a read of whether the target is heard, neither of which waits on the page side's runtime or on a lock the heartbeat or the receive loop holds.

### When a job panics

_(Worker.)_ The station does not set `panic = "abort"`, which the v0 draft did for a process whose only job was manual control. ADR-0016 accepts one process on the condition that each step adding a job keeps it from taking the process down, so manual control's own panics end manual control, not the station, and the heartbeat every vehicle hears goes on:

- **A page connection task** that panics is contained by tokio; control is untouched.
- **The control thread** runs its loop inside `catch_unwind`. On a panic it sends the second of release frames from the catch (Goal 5), prints `control: manual control has ended; restart the station`, and does not run again until the station restarts.
- **The radio side** runs each tick inside `catch_unwind`. On a panic it stops: its liveness mark stops moving, so control treats the radio as vanished (Off within 290 ms, nothing more sent, as Goal 5), and it drops its `watch` sender, which ends each page's feed (`serve` in `src/api.rs`), so no page keeps showing the last input. It prints that the radio side has ended. `main` then waits for a stop signal instead of exiting with it.

The cost: a station that runs on without manual control until restarted, shown only on its terminal until a control view exists. Each case is a test.

### The control thread

The control thread is plain blocking code with no async runtime. It wakes on fixed deadlines, each 40 ms after the last deadline rather than after the last wake-up, so it does not drift, and it never waits on the page side. At each deadline it reads the newest snapshot and moves the state machine. Missed deadlines are skipped, never sent as a burst. The radio counts as lost when no radio is open, when the radio side's liveness mark has not moved for 250 ms (a stalled radio side) and when the thread wakes more than 250 ms after its deadline (a frozen or starved process), so a station that resumes after a freeze comes back in Waiting, not On. A stop signal reaches the control thread directly, through a flag set by the station's own signal handler, never through the page side; the station closes its link and exits only after the control thread has sent its releases. The 25 Hz rate is this spec's; GCS-42 measures it against M1 and M2.

What leaves on each tick, by rung:

- **GCS-41:** one frame on each move into On, and none on the ticks after it. A disarmed vehicle holds it for 3 s and lets it lapse.
- **GCS-42 on:** one frame on every tick while On, and release frames for the second after a deliberate stop (Goal 5), none otherwise. M7's "0 frames while control is disabled" is then measured from one second after a deliberate stop; whether the owner reads M7 that way is an open decision for the owner.

### Frames the station never sends

- **Re-centred sticks from a vanishing radio.** When a joystick is removed, SDL re-centres every input just before it reports the removal (`SDL_PrivateJoystickForceRecentering`, SDL 3.4.20), and both arrive in the same update. The radio side does not drain the queue before a snapshot: it stops taking events at the tick's deadline, however many are still queued (`libs/radio-input/src/pump.rs`), so in real time a tick can end between the re-centring and the removal. What keeps those values off the wire is the queue check: before it publishes, the radio side checks the queue for a removal of the open radio, and if one is queued it handles it first and publishes the radio as gone. Nothing else does: SDL sends every re-centred axis before it releases any button, so a tick that ends mid-burst holds re-centred axes with `control` still on. Tests on the virtual clock end a tick between the burst's axis events and its button events with the removal still queued, and between the burst and its removal, and check that neither publishes the radio open with re-centred values or sends a control frame.
- **A frame to a system id other than the target**, or to system 0.
- **A frame in the arming pose** (Goal 6).

### Decision: `MANUAL_CONTROL`, not `RC_CHANNELS_OVERRIDE`

The v0 draft chose `RC_CHANNELS_OVERRIDE` because the radio delivered channels and its switches mattered as much as its sticks, and with no mapping layer the vehicle's channel settings gave them meaning. The mapping layer reverses both reasons. The station now knows roll, pitch, throttle and yaw by name, calibrated, so `MANUAL_CONTROL`'s four named axes say exactly that, and ArduPilot scales them into its own channel calibration and `RCMAP`, so nothing about the vehicle's RC setup leaks into the station. The switches become commands, which name what they do (Non-goals), instead of PWM on a channel whose meaning lives in the vehicle's parameters. `MANUAL_CONTROL` is also what a gamepad sends, so the second kind of controller needs no second message.

What it costs: the radio's switches do nothing in gcscode until a command rung lands. Releasing is not lost: an axis at `INT16_MAX` releases it as `RC_CHANNELS_OVERRIDE`'s 0 does (the next decision). Revisit if a rung needs a channel `MANUAL_CONTROL` lacks, such as a gimbal axis.

### Decision: a vanished radio stops, a deliberate stop releases

GCS-95 left this to GCS-41: when the radio disappears mid-control, does the station stop sending, send a final neutral frame, or hand over to the vehicle's own failsafe, and how fast? The owner's comment on GCS-43 answers it: "This ticket's behaviour (stop sending and hand over to the vehicle's failsafe) is the right answer", and GCS-43's ticket asks that "sending stops within one loop period". So for a vanished radio the station stops within one tick and sends nothing more. _(Owner.)_

A release would hand the sticks back to a receiver at once instead of after up to 3 s of stale ones, and the worker would have chosen it for a vanished radio too. That is an open decision for the owner, not a default this spec takes over the owner's answer. For every deliberate stop, where the owner's answer says nothing, the station sends the release: _(Worker.)_

- **A neutral frame** was set aside: a centred throttle holds height in Loiter or AltHold but not in Stabilize, and the station does not know the mode's class yet ([ADR-0018](../decisions/ADR-0018-vehicle-classes-as-selectors.md)).
- **A mode change** was set aside: it is a command, which no rung sends yet.

A second of release frames, as the v0 draft sent overrides' releases, because UDP can drop one. The station does not change the vehicle's parameters. GCS-43 reads SITL's `RC_CHANNELS` during the hold after an unplug and during a release, and once with SITL's receiver turned off (`SIM_RC_FAIL`), since SITL always has a receiver otherwise; GCS-44, the first armed rung, judges in flight what each hand-over does, and revisits this decision if it is not acceptable.

### Decision: a replay build refuses a vehicle it cannot show is SITL

The [virtual radio spec](2026-11-24-virtual-radio.md#known-unknowns) left this to GCS-41: `gcscode-station-replay` (GCS-96) sends whatever the station sends, driven by a recording instead of a hand. It must refuse any vehicle it has not shown to be SITL. A recording replayed at a real vehicle flies it blind, with nobody's hand on the sticks.

The tell is a parameter only the simulator has: ArduPilot builds its `SIM_` parameters only when the simulator is compiled in (`AP_SIM_ENABLED`, which SITL turns on). The replay build reads `SIM_SPEEDUP` from the target with `PARAM_REQUEST_READ` before its first control frame to it, and sends control frames only once a `PARAM_VALUE` for it has come back from that system id. It reads it again each time the target is heard again after not being heard, since another vehicle may come back under the same id (1 is ArduPilot's default). It sends none to a vehicle that answers with an error or does not answer within 3 s, and prints why. Other tells were weighed and set aside: the link's address (MAVProxy on localhost can forward a real vehicle), the heartbeat (it does not say SITL) and `AUTOPILOT_VERSION` (it names the board, and needs a request too).

Reading a parameter is the station's first request to a vehicle, so it belongs to a rung of its own: the replay binary sends no control frame at all until that rung lands, and a test that links the player sends only to its fake vehicle (Goal 8). GCS-96's _Done when_ needs no frame: it shows a replay in the Radio input view and keeps the replay off a real radio. So the ladder for replayed runs is GCS-41, then GCS-96, then the `SIM_SPEEDUP` rung, blocked by both, and only then can GCS-42 to GCS-44's checks repeat from a recording. The shipped build makes no such check (Non-goals).

## Validation

### Automated

Rust, each with an absolute expected value:

- **Scaling.** Roll at -100%, -50%, 0%, 50% and 100% gives `y` -1000, -500, 0, 500 and 1000; pitch the same into `x`; yaw into `r`; throttle at 0%, 50% and 100% gives `z` 0, 500 and 1000. A raw value between two whole percents gives the unrounded scaling, not the rounded percent's.
- **A profile missing a stick**, and one that maps no `control`, never leaves Off.
- **The state machine**, one test per event in Goal 4: starting with the switch on, with the sticks out and with them safe (Waiting either way, and nothing sent until off then on); off then on with the sticks safe (On); off then on with throttle at 6% or roll at 11% (Sticks out, then On once the sticks are safe); the radio lost while On (Off within one tick, nothing sent after); the radio back with the switch on and the sticks safe (Waiting, nothing sent until off then on); the target not heard for 3 s (Off, with releases); the target heard again with the switch on and the sticks safe (Waiting); no `--control-vehicle` (Off).
- **The control thread** on a fake clock: 25 deadlines a second with no drift; a liveness mark that stops for 250 ms, and a 1 s freeze, each end in Waiting with no burst of missed ticks; a radio held still for 1 s stays On while a page gets only its one-second repeat; a receive loop stalled for 1 s leaves the deadlines alone; GCS-41's one frame per move into On; from GCS-42, 25 release frames, all four axes at `INT16_MAX`, to the last target after each deliberate stop, a stop signal included, and none after a vanished radio; going On within the release second ends the releases.
- **Panics**: a page connection task that panics leaves control On; a control thread that panics sends its releases, ends manual control and leaves the station running; a radio side that panics stops its liveness mark and its page feeds, control goes Off with nothing more sent, and the station keeps running until a stop signal.
- **Stopping**: `tests/process.rs` sends SIGTERM to the built station while control is On against a fake vehicle, and sees 25 releases, then exit status 0.
- **The arming pose**: throttle 4% with yaw 51% sends nothing; throttle 6% with yaw 100% sends; throttle 0% with yaw 49% sends.
- **The frame**: message 69, the target's system id in `target`, the station's own system id as sender, `buttons` 0.
- **A vanishing radio**: a tick that ends between the re-centring's axis and button events, and one between the re-centring and the removal, each with the removal still queued; neither publishes the radio open with re-centred values or sends a control frame ([Frames the station never sends](#frames-the-station-never-sends)).
- **Test builds**: every test that replays a recording into the station and sends a control frame sends it to its own fake vehicle.

## Known unknowns

- **ArduPilot's arming pose thresholds.** Goal 6's 5% throttle and ±50% yaw are wider than ArduPilot's own rule (throttle at zero within its deadzone, yaw near full, for 3 s), on purpose. GCS-42's live run checks by hand with the real radio that a disarmed SITL stays disarmed in each pose; replaying them waits for the `SIM_SPEEDUP` rung.
- **How the station's frames reach the wire probe.** MAVProxy never passes what one output sends to another ([`tools/wire-probe`](../../tools/wire-probe/README.md#what-it-sees-on-sitl)), so the probe sees the station's frames only through the link GCS-30 builds for the heartbeat, which must show the station's heartbeat on the probe first. GCS-41's proof rests on GCS-30's.
- **That `MANUAL_CONTROL` lands on SITL's channels as the source says.** GCS-41 reads `RC_CHANNELS` for vehicle 1 during its frame's 3 s hold. Where its sticks allow it (they are near centre with throttle low), GCS-42 checks with the stream that roll at 100% puts channel 1 at `RC1_MAX` and pitch at 100% puts channel 2 at `RC2_MIN`.
- **The sign of the profile's pitch.** Goal 2 sends pitch at 100% as `x` 1000, which MAVLink reads as forward. Whether `gcscode-adv`'s pitch reads 100% with the stick forward is checked against the radio in GCS-41's smoke test; if it reads 100% with the stick back, the profile, not the frame, is fixed.
- **The station's system id must be one the vehicle counts as its GCS.** Otherwise ArduPilot drops every frame without a word. GCS-28 picks the id; if it is not 255, the dev stack sets `MAV_GCS_SYSID` to match, or GCS-41 shows frames on the wire that the vehicle ignores.
- **`SIM_` parameters outside SITL.** ArduPilot can build its simulator into flight hardware for testing; such a board would pass Goal 8's tell. It simulates its own flight, so this is accepted. The rung that reads `SIM_SPEEDUP` checks that a non-SITL ArduPilot build has no such parameter.
- **The hand-over after a stop.** What SITL's channels do during the hold after an unplug and after a release, with SITL's simulated receiver behind them and with it turned off (`SIM_RC_FAIL`), is GCS-43's to record; what the vehicle does in flight is GCS-44's.
- **Left for the owner when this round closed:** whether a vanished radio should also release the sticks, against the owner's answer on GCS-43 (PR #117 RT-14); and whether M7's "0 frames while control is disabled" is measured from one second after a deliberate stop.
